# weekly plan

**Shapes the week around what has to be true by Friday, not around the calendar that filled itself.**

Forty hours, thirty meetings, every one legitimate - and not one account is in a different position on Friday than it was on Monday. Reactive work has a deadline attached and proactive work doesn't, so reactive wins every collision, every week, and a calendar left alone fills with other people's priorities by default.

This skill starts from the account list `book-triage` produces, since which accounts deserve the time and how the hours get arranged are different questions. It forces three verifiable outcomes for Friday instead of a task list - a task can be completed and change nothing, an outcome can't - works backwards from them, pushing anything needing another person to Monday, and protects one real, named, two-hour block for work with no deadline, because "proactive time" is what an empty block gets called right before it's given away.

The failure this exists to prevent: **the full week where nothing moved: forty hours, thirty meetings, every one of them legitimate, and not one account in a different position on Friday than it was on Monday.**

Part of the **Run the book** group in [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it actually does

| Step | What you get |
| :--- | :--- |
| Decide the accounts before the hours | The triage list brought over from `book-triage`, or a quick rough version of it, so the week gets built on the right accounts before it gets built at all |
| Write what has to be true by Friday | Three outcomes, not tasks - each one something a colleague could verify without asking how it went |
| Work backwards from the outcomes | Everything needing someone else sent out on Monday, the one real blocker per outcome identified, and the hard conversation scheduled early instead of avoided until Thursday |
| Protect one block for work with no deadline | A specific, named, two-hour block treated like a real meeting - the place the silent account and the account plan actually get touched |
| Batch the reactive load | Two or three windows a day for email, tickets and Slack, with a stated response expectation, instead of continuous interruption fragmenting every hour |
| Cut the calendar honestly | A decline list - recurring calls with no outcome, meetings where you're an observer, meetings that exist to prep for other meetings - each declined hour named as a trade for Step 3 |
| Leave the week some slack | Roughly a fifth of the week left unscheduled as absorption capacity, not free time |
| Close the week | A fifteen-minute Friday review: what moved, what didn't and why, what's carrying and whether it should be |

---

## Who this is for

CSMs and account managers whose week keeps filling with legitimate meetings while proactive work never gets its turn - anyone who says "I'm drowning," "everything is reactive," or "my calendar is full" and means it as a complaint about the week's shape, not its length.

---

## What this needs

**Minimum:** your calendar and a sense of what's urgent - enough to produce outcomes, a protected block, and a decline list.

**Better with:** the output of `book-triage`, which answers which accounts deserve the time. Without it, this skill arranges hours around whatever is loudest, which is the exact problem it exists to solve.

**Best with:** an honest record of where last week actually went, because almost nobody's estimate of that survives contact with the evidence.

Missing context never blocks this skill - it leans on an `account-context` document less than most, and runs fine on just a calendar and a rough sense of priority.


1. Download this repository as a ZIP, or clone it

3. In Claude, go to Customize, then Skills, then Create skill, then Upload skill
4. Upload the ZIP

## What good looks like

- The three outcomes were decided before the calendar was looked at
- Everything needing someone else went out on Monday
- The protected block survived the week and had a specific name
- At least one recurring meeting was declined, shortened or converted
- A fifth of the week was unscheduled and it absorbed what arrived
- Friday's review said what did not move and why, not just what did
