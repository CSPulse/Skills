# internal escalation

**Get your own company to act - with a name, an action, and a date, not a request for attention.**

"Can someone please look at this" names no decision, no deadline, no owner, so it lands at the bottom of a queue sorted by things it doesn't have. This is escalation in the other direction - not the customer escalating to you, but you escalating inside your own company, to people who didn't choose this account and are measured on something else entirely.

This skill starts by naming what you're actually asking for - a decision, work, presence, or just cover - since those go to completely different people, and most failed escalations are a request for work sent to someone who can only give a decision. It finds who actually holds the authority rather than who's senior, shapes the same facts differently for engineering, leadership, support and sales, and refuses to inflate severity, since that trick works once and the cost is paid by every colleague who escalates after you. It also insists you don't disappear once it lands - escalation is not delegation, and the CSM stays the single point of contact throughout.

The failure this exists to prevent: **"can someone please look at this,"** which names no decision, no deadline and no owner, so it goes to the bottom of a queue sorted by things it does not have.

Part of the **Work it internally** group in [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it actually does

| Step | What you get |
| :--- | :--- |
| Decide what you're asking for | The right category - decision, work, presence, or cover - because each goes to a different person and most failures are a mismatch here |
| Find who can actually say yes | The named owner of the specific authority, what they're measured on this quarter, and whether they can decide alone |
| Shape it for the audience | A version tailored to engineering, leadership, support, or sales - the same facts, translated into what each can act on |
| Write the ask as one sentence | "I need [named person or team] to [specific action] by [date], because [what happens otherwise]," plus what you've already tried and why the deadline is real |
| Refuse to inflate | Severity that matches your company's own definition, an honest read of the account's actual importance, and no invented urgency |
| Go one rung at a time | Starting at the lowest rung that could solve it, a stated deadline per rung, and never escalating over someone without telling them first |
| Own it afterward | Staying the customer's single point of contact, keeping the internal owner supplied, and closing the loop with everyone who helped - including when the answer is no |

---

## Who this is for

CSMs and account managers who need engineering, product, leadership, or another internal team to act on a customer's behalf and have been burned before by an escalation that went nowhere, or by one that worked once and cost them credibility for the next three. It's also useful as a gut check before firing off a message in frustration - the four-category split alone catches a lot of misdirected asks.

---

## What this needs

**Minimum:** what you need, and by when. Enough to identify the decider, shape the ask, and write it.

**Better with:** the account's contract value, the commitment already made to the customer, and what you've already tried - since that's the first question you'll be asked.

**Best with:** your company's actual escalation process, including who owns the area and what severity definitions mean there, because using the existing path rather than going around it is most of what makes this work.

Missing context never blocks this skill - where an `account-context` document doesn't exist, it carries on and names the assumption instead.

## Install

**The easy way: one paste**

```
I want to install the internal-escalation skill from
https://github.com/CSPulse/customer-success-skills. Download or clone the
repository, then copy the skills/internal-escalation folder into
~/.claude/skills/ (or .claude/skills/ if this is for one project only),
keeping its own folder name. Tell me the exact folder path it landed in
when you are done.
```

**In the Claude app (no terminal needed)**

1. Download this repository as a ZIP, or clone it
2. Zip the `skills/internal-escalation` folder on its own
3. In Claude, go to Customize, then Skills, then Create skill, then Upload skill
4. Upload the ZIP

**As a plugin, in Claude Code or Cowork**

```
/plugin marketplace add CSPulse/customer-success-skills
/plugin install customer-success-skills@cspulse
```

**Want the whole set?** The [main README's install section](../../#readme) installs all 32 skills at once.
