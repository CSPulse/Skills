# hard conversation

**Say it first, plainly, before someone else says it for you.**

The hard part was never the conversation. It's the four days before it, while the situation quietly worsens and the customer finds out from a status page, their finance team, or a colleague instead of from you - at which point the conversation stops being about the news and becomes about why you said nothing.

This skill makes the actual conversation short: separate what's true from what you merely assume before you speak, decide what you can offer and on whose authority before you're improvising it live, and write the literal opening two sentences so the news lands in line one instead of paragraph four after a note of appreciation for the partnership. It covers four shapes of bad news - a price increase, a roadmap no, a missed commitment, a costly failure, each needing its own opening and concession ladder - and plans for the three ways a customer actually reacts: anger, cold silence, or straight into negotiation.

The failure this exists to prevent: **the runway** - three paragraphs of context and gratitude before the actual news in the fourth, where the customer knew by the second sentence, spent the rest of it waiting, and now trusts the delivery less than the news itself.

Part of the **Handle the conversation** group in [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

---

## What it actually does

| Step | What you get |
| :--- | :--- |
| Decide whether it's happening today | The default answer (yes), and the only two legitimate reasons to wait - each with a deadline attached |
| Get straight what's true and what you can offer | Three honest lists: what's true, what's not known yet (and when it will be), and what you can offer and on whose authority - settled before the call, not during it |
| Pick the channel and the room | Voice before writing, who from your side needs to be briefed rather than just present, and telling your champion before their executive |
| Write the opening two sentences | The literal words: the news plainly in sentence one, their impact in their terms in sentence two, then stop |
| Set the concession ladder and the walk-away | What you'll offer first, what's second and its trigger, what you won't give, and the point where you stop conceding |
| Plan the silence and the three reactions | A prepared line for anger, for cold silence (the dangerous one, often misread as "that went well"), and for straight-to-negotiation |
| Write the record | A same-day, forward-safe written account - no softer than what was said out loud, and nothing new |

---

## Who this is for

CSMs and account managers who owe a customer bad news and have been finding reasons to delay it - a price increase to deliver, a feature request to turn down, a missed deadline to own, or an outage with a real cost attached. It's equally useful as a pre-flight check when you already know what you're going to say but haven't decided what you can actually offer, or on whose authority.

---

## What this needs

**Minimum:** what happened, and who it affects. Enough to produce the opening lines, the likely reactions, and the written record.

**Better with:** the commitment as it was actually made, in writing - almost every disagreement in these conversations is about what was promised, and the sentence that settles it is usually in an email nobody has reread.

**Best with:** the contract, the account plan, and a current risk read, because those decide how much of what you're about to say is a commercial event rather than a service one.

Missing context never blocks this skill - where an `account-context` document doesn't exist, it carries on and names the assumption instead.

## Install

**The easy way: one paste**

```
I want to install the hard-conversation skill from
https://github.com/CSPulse/customer-success-skills. Download or clone the
repository, then copy the skills/hard-conversation folder into
~/.claude/skills/ (or .claude/skills/ if this is for one project only),
keeping its own folder name. Tell me the exact folder path it landed in
when you are done.
```

**In the Claude app (no terminal needed)**

1. Download this repository as a ZIP, or clone it
2. Zip the `skills/hard-conversation` folder on its own
3. In Claude, go to Customize, then Skills, then Create skill, then Upload skill
4. Upload the ZIP

**As a plugin, in Claude Code or Cowork**

```
/plugin marketplace add CSPulse/customer-success-skills
/plugin install customer-success-skills@cspulse
```

**Want the whole set?** The [main README's install section](../../#readme) installs all 32 skills at once.

**Or just read it.** `SKILL.md` is the method, and `references/by-scenario.md` breaks down the four situations - commercial change, roadmap no, missed commitment, costly failure - in more depth than the general method needs to cover.
