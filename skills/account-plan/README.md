# account plan

**Write the plan someone else can pick up in month four and actually use.**

Most account plans get written once, for a review, and never opened again - twelve initiatives, no measure, no owner on the customer's side, and no way for the plan to ever be wrong. A document that can't be failed is never really achieved.

The test this skill applies: could someone inheriting the account in month four read it and know what was promised, by whom, and what's supposed to be true by when? So it insists on an objective in the customer's own words with a baseline and target, owners named on both sides, risks that carry a trigger instead of a worry, and no more than five actions - because past that, nothing is actually prioritized.

The failure this exists to prevent: **the plan full of activity - twelve initiatives, no measure, no owners on the customer side, and no way to be wrong. It cannot be failed, so it is never achieved.**

Part of the **Run the book** group in [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

---

## What it actually does

| Step | What you get |
| :--- | :--- |
| Step 1: The objective, in their words | One or two objectives with a baseline, a target, and a named owner on the customer's side |
| Step 2: Where the account actually is | Four honest lines: what's working, what isn't, what changed, what you don't know |
| Step 3: The people summary | Decider, champion, sceptic, the biggest relationship gap, and a route to closing it |
| Step 4: A short action list | Three to five actions, each with an owner, a date, and a stated so-what - some of them the customer's |
| Step 5: Risks with triggers | What would tell you a risk is happening, what you'd do, and by when you need to know |
| Step 6: Written for a successor | Everything dated, every commitment attributed, inference marked as inference |
| Step 7: A review trigger, not just a cadence | The events - sponsor change, contraction, missed measure, reorg - that force an update regardless of schedule |

## Who this is for

Anyone who owns a strategic or durable plan for a named account - not every account in the book, only the ones `book-triage` decides earn one. It's built for the moment an account changes hands: a CSM leaving, a book reshuffle, a new hire inheriting a portfolio, or a QBR where the plan itself gets checked rather than just referenced.

It is not the plan for someone's first ninety days on an account (that's `onboarding-plan`), and it is not the business-wide substrate every skill reads (that's `account-context`). This is the twelve-month picture of one specific account.

---

## What this needs

**Minimum:** the account, its contract, and what you know about the customer's goals - enough for a plan with honestly marked gaps.

**Better with:** the original business case, current outcome data, and a stakeholder picture, which together supply the objective, the measure, and the owners.

**Best with:** the customer's own stated goals, agreed with them directly rather than inferred. A plan written about a customer is weaker than one written with them, and the gap shows up at the renewal.

Missing context never blocks this skill - where a measure can't be stated, that becomes the first named gap and agreeing it becomes the first action, rather than the plan quietly working around the hole.

## What this does not do

- It does not write plans for a whole book. `book-triage` decides which accounts earn one, and writing plans for all forty is how the format loses credibility.
- It does not duplicate the stakeholder map. It summarises the decider, champion, sceptic, and biggest gap, and points to `stakeholder-map` for the full picture.
- It does not treat a quarterly cadence as sufficient. It sets review triggers - a sponsor change, a contraction, a missed measure - that force an update regardless of when the next scheduled review falls.
