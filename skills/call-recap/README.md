# call recap

**A recorded call is not one email. It's a set of them, and the ones you forget are the expensive ones.**

This failure is quiet and easy to miss: you write a tidy recap to the coordinator, feel done, and leave the three emails that actually move the account unwritten. Weeks later the introduction that was promised never happened, the security note the CISO was waiting on never arrived, and there's no record of why.

This skill treats the call recording as a source, not a summary to restate. It pulls the full transcript instead of the AI overview, because a summary keeps the categories and drops the specifics that decide how you open the next demo. It finds every email the call actually generated by scanning for commitments rather than topics ("I'll send you," "can you introduce me"), groups them by recipient, and grounds every claim in a draft back to a real source - the transcript for what was said, the mailbox and account plan for what's already true, a human for anything about the product. Anything it can't trace gets flagged, not asserted.

The failure this exists to prevent: **writing a tidy recap to the coordinator, feeling done, and leaving the three emails that actually move the account unwritten.**

Part of the **Handle the conversation** group in [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it actually does

| Step | What you get |
| :--- | :--- |
| Step 0-1: Orient and get the full transcript | The complete transcript, not just the AI summary, plus a note on what house style or voice guide was found |
| Step 2: Load the account | Stakeholders, contract state, open risks, and the prep sheet's original outcome, where an account folder exists |
| Step 3: Find the humans and threads | Real email addresses (never guessed) and the existing threads most follow-ups should reply into |
| Step 4: An honest read of the call | What genuinely moved, what's at risk, what got missed, the sharpest line said, and whether a "block" was ever real |
| Step 5: Ground every claim | Every factual statement traced to its source - transcript, mailbox, account plan, or a human - before it reaches a draft |
| Step 6: Plan the email set | Every commitment turned into an email, grouped by recipient, ordered by dependency and timing |
| Step 7: Write the drafts | Full drafts in a working document, each flagged for grounding gaps, product claims, ownership, or missing data |
| Step 8: Stage the drafts | Clean drafts staged in the mail client where connected; flagged ones held and named; the user always sends |
| Step 9: Hygiene pass | New stakeholders, plan reversals, and stale commitments surfaced for the account record |

---

## Who this is for

Anyone who just came out of a customer call and needs to turn it into what actually gets sent - a CSM working from a Zoom or Gong recording, a pasted transcript, or just a meeting summary someone forwarded. It's recorder-agnostic: it works the same whether or not a connector is available, just with more of the legwork automated when one is.

---

## What this needs

**Minimum:** a transcript - pasted, uploaded, or pointed at. Everything runs from that, and the output arrives in chat.

**Better with:** a connected meeting recorder to fetch the transcript itself, a connected mailbox to find real addresses and existing threads, and somewhere to write files so the drafts document lives next to the account.

**Best with:** a workspace holding account notes and a voice guide - the difference between a competent recap and one you send without rewriting it.

Missing context never blocks this skill - each missing piece costs one specific, named capability (an address it couldn't find, a claim it couldn't verify, a voice it had to write plainly instead of matching), and it says which one out loud rather than guessing around it.

## What this does not do

- It never sends anything. Clean drafts get staged where a mail connector exists; the user always sends.
- It does not stage a draft carrying an unresolved flag. A flagged draft sitting in an outbox is one keystroke from reaching a customer with an unverified claim in it.
- It does not treat the call as the source for what's already true about the account. What was sent, what was promised in a prior month, what the contract says - that comes from the mailbox and account plan, not from what someone said out loud on the call, because people compress and misremember in exactly the places that matter.
