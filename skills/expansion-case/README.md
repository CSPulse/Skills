# expansion case

**Write the ammunition your champion carries, not the pitch you would give.**

You won't be in the room. Your champion is, presenting to people who've never met you, against three other requests for the same money - and most expansion asks fail before that meeting starts because they were written as a pitch with a vendor logo on it instead of a document built to survive someone else's finance team.

This skill starts by disqualifying most expansions before writing a word: is the existing deployment demonstrably working, in a way the customer would say out loud? If not, asking for more tells the account you weren't paying attention and puts the renewal at risk chasing revenue that was never there. Past that gate, it finds the problem the customer's leadership has already named, traces where the budget actually sits (routinely not your champion's own), and sizes the ask small enough for one person to approve - ending in a one-page, logo-free document with the ask in the first three lines and the two sentences the champion says out loud in a corridor, because those are the part that actually travels.

The failure this exists to prevent: **the beautiful deck that dies in someone's inbox**, because the champion could not defend slide four when their CFO pushed on it.

Part of the **Run the set-piece** group in [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

---

## What it actually does

| Step | What you get |
| :--- | :--- |
| Step 0: earn the ask | A hard stop if the existing deployment isn't demonstrably working - a named outcome a named person on their side would repeat, not "usage is healthy" |
| Step 1: find the anchor | The problem their leadership already agreed exists, in their own words, so the ask competes with nothing instead of every other improvement on the list |
| Step 2: follow the money | Which of four sources the budget actually comes from - an existing line, a new planning-cycle ask, another department's budget, or something it displaces - and who controls it |
| Step 3: the three numbers | Cost all-in, return in their units, and a conservative payback period, with every estimate marked as an estimate and its basis shown |
| Step 4: size it to survive one meeting | A first step small enough for one person to approve, that produces a result inside one cycle, plus the full picture stated honestly alongside it |
| Step 5: pre-answer the objections | The six questions finance and a competing peer will actually ask, answered in the document instead of left to the champion to improvise |
| Step 6: write it for the room you won't be in | Their vocabulary, no product tier names, no logo, one page, and the two sentences that travel by mouth |

---

## Who this is for

CSMs and account managers preparing an upsell, cross-sell, seat expansion, or second-department rollout - anyone whose champion has to defend the ask to people the CSM will never meet. It's equally useful as a gut check before you write anything: Step 0 alone disqualifies a meaningful share of expansion asks that shouldn't be made yet.

---

## What this needs

**Minimum:** what you want to expand into, and who would sponsor it. Enough to produce the case structure, the likely objections, and a first pass at sizing.

**Better with:** current outcome data from the existing deployment, the customer's stated goals, and the original business case - the outcome data is what earns you the right to ask at all.

**Best with:** a stakeholder map, because the budget holder for an expansion is frequently not the person you've been working with, and whether you've actually met them changes the plan.

Missing context never blocks this skill - where an `account-context` document doesn't exist, it carries on and names the assumption instead.

## Install

**The easy way: one paste**

```
I want to install the expansion-case skill from
https://github.com/CSPulse/customer-success-skills. Download or clone the
repository, then copy the skills/expansion-case folder into
~/.claude/skills/ (or .claude/skills/ if this is for one project only),
keeping its own folder name. Tell me the exact folder path it landed in
when you are done.
```

**In the Claude app (no terminal needed)**

1. Download this repository as a ZIP, or clone it
2. Zip the `skills/expansion-case` folder on its own
3. In Claude, go to Customize, then Skills, then Create skill, then Upload skill
4. Upload the ZIP

**As a plugin, in Claude Code or Cowork**

```
/plugin marketplace add CSPulse/customer-success-skills
/plugin install customer-success-skills@cspulse
```

**Want the whole set?** The [main README's install section](../../#readme) installs all 32 skills at once.

**Or just read it.** `SKILL.md` is the method, and `assets/business-case.md` is the one-page template itself - fill it in and hand it to the champion, with or without Claude involved.
